Audio Legend & Sound Mapping

How real NASA climate numbers are translated into audible acoustic frequencies

❄️

Cool Climate Periods

Anomalies below the 1951–1980 baseline (e.g. -0.80°C to -0.20°C in the late 19th and early 20th century) map to deep, resonant lower frequencies (180 Hz – 350 Hz).

Pitch: 180 Hz – 350 Hz
⚖️

Reference Baseline (0.00°C)

The 1951–1980 global mean reference baseline maps precisely to 400 Hz. When anomaly is 0.00°C, the sound rings at this steady anchor pitch.

Reference Pitch: 400 Hz (Baseline)
🔥

Planetary Warming Anomalies

Positive anomalies climbing from +0.50°C to modern record highs (+1.55°C) produce higher, urgent tones (500 Hz – 720 Hz), giving physical acoustic weight to warming.

Pitch: 500 Hz – 720 Hz (Urgent)

NASA Data Sources & Scientific Credits

All sonifications and visualizations are generated strictly from authentic NASA scientific data

NASA GISS GISTEMP v4

Global Land-Ocean Temperature Index: Monthly surface temperature anomalies from 1880 to 2026 relative to the 1951–1980 base period. Combines meteorological station measurements and ocean temperature buoys.

NASA GISS Data Portal →

NASA POWER Meteorology

Prediction of Worldwide Energy Resources: Daily station surface meteorology for Bengaluru (12.9716°N, 77.5946°E) from 2020 to 2025. Parameters include 2-meter air temperature, precipitation, and relative humidity.

NASA POWER API Portal →

NASA Aqua Satellite (AIRS)

Atmospheric Infrared Sounder: High-resolution satellite sounder data validating tropospheric temperature trends and global comparison models from 2002 to 2026.

NASA JPL AIRS Portal →
